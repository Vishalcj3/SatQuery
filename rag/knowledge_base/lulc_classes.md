# BigEarthNet v2.0 — 19-class land-use/land-cover (LULC) nomenclature

Each entry defines a class as used in BigEarthNet.txt annotations. Definitions
follow the CORINE Land Cover (CLC) scheme these classes are derived from.

## Urban fabric
Residential and built-up areas: continuous and discontinuous housing, streets,
and associated green space. Dominated by buildings and impervious surfaces.

## Industrial or commercial units
Factories, warehouses, commercial centres, and their access infrastructure.
Large flat roofs and paved lots distinguish them from residential urban fabric.

## Arable land
Cultivated land for annual crops (cereals, root crops): non-irrigated arable
land, permanently irrigated land, and rice fields. Regular field geometry.

## Permanent crops
Crops not in rotation that occupy the land for long periods: vineyards, fruit
trees and berry plantations, olive groves.

## Pastures
Grassland under agricultural use — grazed or mechanically harvested for hay;
dense grass cover, often with hedgerows or fences.

## Complex cultivation patterns
Mosaics of small parcels of annual crops, pasture, and permanent crops, often
mixed with scattered houses and gardens.

## Land principally occupied by agriculture, with significant areas of natural vegetation
Agricultural land interspersed with substantial natural vegetation (shrubs,
trees, wetland patches).

## Agro-forestry areas
Annual crops or grazing under a tree canopy (e.g. dehesa/montado); combined
agriculture and forestry on the same parcel.

## Broad-leaved forest
Forest of deciduous/broad-leaved species (oak, beech, birch) where tree cover
exceeds shrub and herbaceous cover.

## Coniferous forest
Forest dominated by needle-leaved evergreen species (pine, spruce, fir).

## Mixed forest
Forest where neither broad-leaved nor coniferous species exceed ~75% cover — a
mixture of the two.

## Natural grassland and sparsely vegetated areas
Grassland with low or no human influence, plus areas of sparse vegetation over
rock, sand, or scree (>50% bare).

## Moors, heathland and sclerophyllous vegetation
Low woody vegetation: heaths, moorland, and drought-adapted sclerophyllous
scrub (maquis, garrigue).

## Transitional woodland/shrub
Shrub or young trees regenerating after clearance/fire, or forest degradation —
an intermediate stage between grassland/shrub and forest.

## Beaches, dunes, sands
Unvegetated or sparsely vegetated sand and dune systems along coasts, rivers,
or inland; also referred to as "beaches, dunes, or sands".

## Inland wetlands
Non-forested, seasonally or permanently waterlogged land inland: marshes and
peat bogs.

## Coastal wetlands
Tidal and brackish wetlands: salt marshes, salines, and intertidal flats along
the coast.

## Inland waters
Rivers, canals, lakes, and reservoirs — standing or flowing fresh water inland.
Also called "inland water".

## Marine waters
Open sea, coastal lagoons, and estuaries — saline water bodies connected to the
ocean. Also called "marine waters".
